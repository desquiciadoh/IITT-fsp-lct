Digital Aggregator Platform for IT Vacancies with a Verified Achievement Profile for the Russian Federation of Sports Programming (FSP)Terms of Reference (Technical Specifications) / 2026   1. Context and Problem RelevanceExisting job boards have largely exhausted their utility: they are generic, built for all industries simultaneously, and no longer solve the core problem for either side. Employers are forced to manually sift through hundreds of irrelevant applications using expensive technical specialists, while candidates apply blindly without receiving any feedback. Both parties incur heavy costs, while recruitment efficiency remains minimal.   In the IT industry, the issue is exacerbated by the very nature of qualifications: a formal CV lists stacks and work history but fails to verify practical competence, reducing screening to keyword matching. Domain-specific parameters—tech stack, seniority grade, team role, and work format—are not formalized on generic platforms; consequently, recommendation relevance is poor for both employers and job seekers.   A dedicated service built specifically for IT that communicates with both sides in a native professional language can radically improve recruitment accuracy and significantly shorten hiring cycles.   An added value of such a platform is the verifiable proof of qualifications. The Russian Federation of Sports Programming (FSP) possesses performance data on participants in competitions and events, offering an objective, verifiable track record of practical problem-solving skills. Automatically enriching a candidate’s profile with these achievements provides employers with a reliable evaluation metric and allows competitive programming participants to leverage their tournament track record into a tangible career advantage.   2. Problem DescriptionDevelop a digital web platform for IT talent acquisition featuring a reverse recruitment mechanic: job seekers are automatically sorted into categories based on an objectively verified profile, while the employer browses target categories and directly reaches out to a specific candidate with a concrete job offer and salary figure. The objective goes beyond scraping third-party websites or acting as a job board aggregator: it requires an independent platform establishing a direct connection strictly between employer and candidate.   A candidate’s category visible to employers is defined not by a self-written CV, but through a mandatory onboarding process: an industry/specialization questionnaire, self-assessment of the expected grade, and grade-verification testing.
A category represents a group of homogeneous applicants based on a pair of specialization and grade (e.g., Backend Developers with experience, Frontend Developers with up to 1 year of experience). Within each category, candidates are ranked: higher ranks are awarded to candidates with stronger verified profiles and confirmed participation in FSP competitions and events. The team defines the directory of specializations and grades independently based on standard IT industry definitions—core domains are sufficient for the MVP stage.   The team must design and implement a functional testing engine. A single static question bank for all candidates is unacceptable, as questions and answers will leak quickly across the community; relying solely on neural-network-generated unique tasks for each candidate may address leaks, but fails to ensure comparable difficulty and objective grading across different users. Design and implement an approach resilient to these challenges. Task formats and content are up to the team: coding challenges, questionnaires, or other formats. It is sufficient to prepare a few sample job descriptions and demonstrate how the system dynamically constructs assessments and tests based on them.   Grades are not forcibly downgraded based on test outcomes: a candidate who fails the assessment for their claimed level may take a test for the grade below, whereas a candidate who performs decisively may upgrade their grade. Grade changes must have a time restriction (e.g., no more than once every three months)—the team chooses the specific cooldown interval.   Beyond the initial assessment determining grade and profile, the system incorporates recurring short assignments: periodically offering the candidate an employer-provided micro-task to solve or propose an approach for. This keeps the profile active and provides employers with an up-to-date signal on the candidate.   The central deliverable is designing and implementing a contact engagement flow fundamentally different from traditional direct job applications. A conceptual benchmark (illustrating the direction rather than a direct blueprint) is the model of international platforms such as LHH (Lee Hecht Harrison), where the employer takes the initiative by browsing a pre-categorized pool and issuing an invitation to an individual. This reverse mechanic constitutes the core focus of the hackathon and serves as the primary evaluation criterion.   The candidate’s profile must support linking to an FSP Participant ID. In the future, FSP ID will operate on Keycloak; therefore, architectural compatibility with this solution will facilitate subsequent integration. However, this is an architectural guideline rather than a strict requirement: teams are free to select their own authorization flow, and sound architectural decisions may be adopted officially. No API or data schema for FSP is deliberately provided: teams determine what participant achievement data is worth ingesting and how to surface it to employers. Any schema and presentation approach are acceptable as part of the creative challenge, and successful patterns will inform the production integration. Graceful fallback handling when a candidate lacks an FSP history is strictly required.   There are no pre-packaged candidate/vacancy datasets or golden benchmarks provided for testing accuracy and matching quality. Teams work with their own data—either synthetic or scraped from open sources. Consequently, teams must formulate their own evaluation and validation methodology, detail it in the project deliverables, and present the results. Both the solution and the rigor of its validation system will be judged.   Items marked as beyond the MVP scope represent strategic vectors for future product evolution; teams are expected to deliver conceptual and architectural blueprints rather than working code for these aspects, to be further refined with FSP post-hackathon.   Bonus points will be awarded for:   Fraud-prevention mechanisms against fake vacancies and bad-faith employers.   Integration with internal corporate Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS).   2.1. Functional RequirementsThe MVP realistically deliverable within the hackathon timeframe comprises two role-based dashboards: Candidate and Employer. The minimum baseline feature set is outlined below; teams define interface workflows and concrete implementations independently.   Candidate DashboardProfile & Resume Filling: Manual form entry or PDF resume parsing (full name, contacts, tech stack, grade, roles, years of experience, soft skills); automated generation of a standardized PDF profile.   Skill Assessment: Industry/specialization survey, self-declared grade selection, assessment completion; results automatically assign the visibility category for employers.   Periodic Tasks: The system periodically provides short employer-generated tasks (solve or outline an approach); results maintain profile freshness (desirable, optional for MVP).   Category & Grade Management: Current status view (domain, specialization, grade), test history, ability to retake tests for a higher or lower grade subject to a frequency cooldown.   Review Employer Invitations: View inbound offers displaying the specific vacancy and offered salary level, with options to accept or decline.   Job Browsing & Direct Applications: Public list of vacancies, self-service applications, and application status tracking. This flow is secondary to the primary reverse-recruitment mechanic (desirable, optional for MVP).   Settings: Privacy controls (controlling what details employers see), data processing and publishing consent, linking to FSP Participant ID.   Access Control: Candidates access only their own invitations and applications; peer candidate data remains completely restricted.   Employer DashboardCompany Profile: Corporate summary, business domain, and official contact details.   Hiring Needs Specification: Definition of target roles, team context, required stack, specialization, and seniority grade—used by the system to generate a curated talent pool.   Candidate Pool Management: Browsing recommended categories and individual profiles, along with transparent matching justifications for each candidate.   Candidate Bank Search: Search access across the candidate database with filters by specialization, grade, category, stack, and verified FSP achievements.   Reaching Out / Initiating Contact: Sending direct invitations to specific candidates containing offer details and salary ranges without requiring a published vacancy; tracking invitation statuses.   Vacancy Publishing: Direct posting and editing of job openings, reviewing inbound applicants, and managing candidate pipelines (desirable, optional for MVP).   Access Control: Candidate contact details remain hidden until the candidate accepts an invitation or submits a direct application.   Functional Requirements for Search and MatchingCandidate listings are generated based on verified categories assigned through onboarding surveys and testing, rather than unverified CV claims.   Candidates within a category are ranked by profile strength: candidates with higher test scores and verified FSP achievements rank higher.   Search results are sorted by match relevancy against the employer’s stated requirements, accompanied by clear, explainable match justifications.   Support for dynamic filtering and query refinement without losing the current candidate selection.   2.2. Data Rules and Visibility GovernanceTeams design the database schema and storage models independently. The following mandatory constraints must be enforced:   Mandatory Salary Disclosure: Salary ranges ("from - to" in RUB) must be explicitly stated in both job postings and direct candidate invitations. Candidates must see compensation terms prior to initiating dialogue—a defining differentiator of this platform.   Contact Gating: Invitations must detail the offer description, compensation range, company name, and preferred contact channel. Candidate personal contact details are revealed to the employer only after the candidate accepts the invitation or applies directly.   Status Tracking: Invitations and applications must feature explicit lifecycle statuses (e.g., Sent, Viewed, Accepted, Declined), visible in real-time to both parties.   Registration: Email-based onboarding with address verification. Formal company verification and vacancy moderation are not required for the MVP (classified under post-MVP fraud prevention).   3. Hardware and Software Requirements3.1. Hardware RequirementsNo proprietary hardware is required. The solution must execute reliably on a standard workstation or an entry-level virtual server. When containerized with external dependencies, total memory footprint should stay within reasonable bounds.   3.2. Software Stack RequirementsNo strict technology limitations are imposed; teams select their own tech stack. Section 3.5 provides architectural recommendations and product guidelines rather than binding constraints; reasoned deviations will not penalize project scores. Leveraging Machine Learning (ML) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) models for resume parsing, candidate categorization, and talent matching will be evaluated as a distinct competitive advantage.   3.3. Technical Documentation RequirementsComprehensive accompanying technical documentation is mandatory and must cover:   Functional and component software architecture.   Candidate assessment mechanics, with a rationale for leak-resistance against widespread question distribution.   Matching algorithm and candidate categorization logic.   Team validation methodology and benchmark evaluation results.   Proposed integration schema with the FSP participant registry.   OpenAPI / API documentation.   Bill of materials: libraries, frameworks, components, and exact versions used.   Step-by-step instructions for build, deployment, and local setup.   3.5. Non-Functional and Architectural GuidelinesOpen Source: Preference for open-source licenses (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD) or software from the Russian Unified Register of Domestic Software. Source code must be clean, uncompiled, and un-obfuscated.   Architecture: Clear layer separation between Frontend (web client for both roles), Backend (API business logic), and Data Persistence (relational or document DBMS). Microservices are welcomed; modular monoliths with strict layer isolation and loose coupling are fully acceptable.   Containerization: Packaging services into Docker containers using Dockerfile and docker-compose.yml is strongly encouraged to ensure smooth evaluation. The platform must execute deterministically following the provided manual.   API Standards: All endpoints documented via OpenAPI (Swagger) specifications, detailing request payloads, response schemas, and HTTP error codes.   Security & Compliance: Alignment with Russian Federal Law No. 152-FZ (Personal Data Protection). Clear user consent mechanisms for processing and publishing profile data, strict role-based access control (RBAC), unauthorized access protection, and secure hashing/storage of credentials and session tokens.   Authentication: Future compatibility with Keycloak (the target standard for FSP ID) is highly recommended to streamline post-hackathon handover, though teams retain the choice of auth engine.   Cross-Platform Compatibility: Cross-browser web application support on modern desktop browsers, completely OS-agnostic deployment. Adaptive mobile/tablet layout is optional for the MVP.   Performance Baseline (MVP): Reliable concurrent handling of dozens of simultaneous active sessions; low latency on primary operations (profile loads, candidate search, recommendation pool generation).   Code Quality: Meaningful code comments on complex logic, strict adherence between code and architecture documentation, clean reproducible builds; the team must be prepared to run and demonstrate the code upon jury request.   4.
